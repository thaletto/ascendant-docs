Western astrology is a family of Hellenistic, medieval, Renaissance,
traditional, and modern practices. Most contemporary Western charts use the
tropical zodiac, but zodiac choice alone does not define the tradition.
Western sidereal astrologers also exist.

## The shared natal-chart grammar

A Western natal reading usually combines:

- planets, including Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto in many modern schools,
- zodiacal signs,
- twelve houses,
- angular relationships called aspects,
- planetary rulership and dignity,
- the Ascendant, Midheaven, and other angles,
- and sometimes calculated points such as the lunar nodes or lots.

Traditional and modern schools can assign different emphasis or meaning to the
same factor. A reproducible reading should identify its school, zodiac, house
system, aspect set, rulership scheme, and allowed points.

## Aspects

An aspect measures angular separation. The major Ptolemaic aspects are:

| Aspect | Separation |
| --- | --- |
| Conjunction | 0° |
| Sextile | 60° |
| Square | 90° |
| Trine | 120° |
| Opposition | 180° |

An **orb** defines how far from exact an aspect may be while still being
counted. Schools differ on orb size, whether the aspect is applying or
separating, and which minor aspects are included. Those choices should be
inputs, not hidden assumptions.

Ptolemy's *Tetrabiblos* is an important primary witness for the classical
planet, sign, and aspect framework; the
[Loeb translation is available through LacusCurtius](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Ptolemy/Tetrabiblos/home.html).

## Dignity, rulership, and condition

Traditional judgment asks whether a planet can act according to its nature and
whether it has authority over the matter being judged. Sign rulership,
exaltation, angularity, sect, speed, visibility, aspects, and house placement
can all contribute. Modern psychological schools often place more emphasis on
symbolic integration and personal experience.

Neither approach is captured by a list of generic planet keywords. The chart
condition must be evaluated under one declared method.

## Houses and angles

The Ascendant and Midheaven are location-dependent angles. House systems divide
the local sphere or related arcs in different ways. Placidus, Porphyry, Equal,
and Whole Sign houses can place the same planet in different houses without
changing its zodiacal longitude.

Swiss Ephemeris documents the supported
[house calculation methods](https://www.astro.com/swisseph/swisseph.htm).

## Western timing

Common Western timing tools include:

- transits,
- secondary progressions,
- solar-arc directions,
- annual profections,
- solar returns,
- and other traditional time-lord techniques.

These are not interchangeable with a planetary dasha. A transit follows the
current sky; a progression or direction advances the natal figure by a defined
symbolic rate; a profection assigns annual emphasis through a counting rule.

## Relationship to Jyotisha

Western and Indian astrology share ancient astronomical and horoscopic
vocabulary, but their later rule systems are not identical. Differences can
include zodiac choice, nodes and outer planets, aspect doctrine, house
lordship, divisional charts, nakshatras, dashas, yogas, and the order of
judgment.

Ascendant currently focuses on sidereal Jyotisha calculations and a
Parashari–Raman agent workflow. Its support for Placidus or Porphyry houses does
not make it a complete Western astrology interpreter.
